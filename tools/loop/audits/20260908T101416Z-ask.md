## Prompt

A game hides a structured layer the player must infer from indirect clues. Research shows habitual gamblers detect illusory patterns in random data more than others, so hidden-structure games risk preying on that. The proposed safeguard: measure the mutual information between the hidden layer and the observable clues, require it above a threshold, and reveal the true hidden layer after every round so players learn whether their inference was correct. Name the strongest way this safeguard still fails to protect vulnerable players.

## Auditor response

The safeguard fails by facilitating **reinforcement of the illusion of control**. By providing immediate feedback on whether an inference was correct, the system transforms a stochastic process into a reinforcement learning task. For vulnerable players, this feedback loop validates their pattern-seeking behavior, converting a game of chance into a perceived game of skill. This increases engagement and the psychological drive to "master" the structure, which is more addictive than pure randomness.

**Condition:** This failure bites when the feedback loop rewards successful inferences, as it provides the neurological reinforcement necessary to solidify the player's belief that the hidden layer is predictable and controllable.
