## Prompt

A puzzle game's scoring rule was changed to fix a measured defect. OLD RULE: banking a cell scores its own face value times (1 + charge on it). Measurement showed a greedy 'pick the biggest number' strategy beat a random strategy by 79.8%, and that this advantage did NOT shrink when the game's hidden link structure was replaced with uniform noise (83.4%) - so the hidden structure was contributing nothing. NEW RULE: banking a cell scores the face of the cell it secretly FEEDS, times (1 + charge on the banked cell); a cell with a dead link scores only its own face. After the change: greedy beats random by 21.4%; a strategy that models the hidden structure beats greedy by 26.3%; and on a scrambled structure that same modelling strategy becomes 22.0% WORSE than greedy. Name the single strongest objection to concluding from these numbers that the game now rewards skill. Be brief and concrete. 2600

## Auditor response

The "skill" is actually overfitting. The modeling strategy's performance falls significantly below the greedy baseline when the structure is scrambled, proving the advantage is a fragile exploitation of a specific topology rather than a robust, generalizable strategic proficiency.
