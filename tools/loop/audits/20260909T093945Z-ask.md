## Prompt

Project A is a research repo with a genetic-evolution 'foundry': it defines a Genome with structural and expression loci, a novelty admission gate, branch weights, and a fitness function scoring telemetry plus human 'Delphi' judgment. It cannot prove any bred offspring is fair or replayable. Project B is a shipping Android dice game with 24 verification oracles: uniform-dice chi-square, a semantic rules hash pinned into the APK, commit-reveal, a measured inference-value check, and leak checks over DOM and GPU buffers. I plan to make B supersede A by breeding genomes and admitting an offspring ONLY when B's oracle suite passes on it — measurement instead of opinion. The gene pool: 311 of 1116 image-generation prompts in the shared corpus carry explicit {allele|allele} wildcard syntax, giving 1026 loci and 581 distinct alleles, mean 3.3 loci per prompt. I already conceded these alleles are visual ('neon cyan', 'wild tile') so they can found only the EXPRESSION loci, never the structural ones, and that the corpus's human ratings are all zero so the judgment half of fitness has no input at all. Given those two concessions, is 'admit by oracle suite' actually a stronger selection mechanism than a fitness function, or does it fail in a way I have not named?

## Auditor response

It fails due to **domain mismatch**; the oracle suite provides no **fitness gradient** for the target domain. Project B’s oracles are designed to verify the stochastic integrity and security of a dice game (e.g., chi-square uniformity, buffer leaks), which are orthogonal to the semantic or aesthetic quality of visual expression loci. Because the oracles cannot measure the "goodness" or "fitness" of a visual prompt, they act as a stochastic filter rather than an evolutionary driver. They will pass or fail offspring based on properties unrelated to the visual alleles, effectively turning the breeding process into a random walk through the prompt space rather than a directed optimization.
