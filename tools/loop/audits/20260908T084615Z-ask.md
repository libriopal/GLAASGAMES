## Prompt

A game's simulation state is genuinely four-dimensional: entities have x, y, z and w positions, and the w axis is load-bearing — a target at your exact x, y, z can be unreachable because it is far away in w. The designers now want the player to see only a 2D lens: a flat presentation that summarises the 4D state, because 4D is too much information for a person. The 4D structure stays as the backend. State the strongest argument that this 2D lens cannot be an honest representation, and the specific situation where it misleads the player.

## Auditor response

The strongest argument is the mathematical impossibility of an injective mapping from $\mathbb{R}^4$ to $\mathbb{R}^2$; dimensionality reduction necessitates information loss, meaning distinct 4D states will inevitably map to identical 2D coordinates. This misleads the player during **false proximity events**: specifically, when an entity and a target share the same 2D projection but possess divergent $w$ values, the player will perceive a collision or an accessible target that is actually unreachable or physically distant in the simulation.
