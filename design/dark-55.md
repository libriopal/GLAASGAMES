# Darkmatter — 55 candidates, against four measured problems

Not a wish list. EINCOL round 1 produced four specific problems, and every
candidate below is a proposed solution to one of them. Each is marked **[cheap]**
if it can be tested with what already exists.

| # | the problem, as measured |
|---|---|
| **P1** | "Expansion" is not measurable yet — sd-of-outcome measures *lever strength*, and DRAG (a stabiliser) topped the table because of it |
| **P2** | Every verb, and doing nothing well, pushes boundness the SAME way (0.936 / 0.927 / 0.764) — no tension |
| **P3** | A 3D system must be legible in a still frame; the camera had no elevation and a plane projected to a line |
| **P4** | The reachable set may be large in coordinates no player can perceive — "a dataset, not a game" |

## A · Measuring expansion honestly (P1) — 1–12

1. **Vector outcome, volume measure.** [cheap] Replace the scalar with
   (bound fraction, angular momentum, radial spread, cluster count, max
   separation); expansion = volume of the convex hull of reachable outcomes.
2. **Rank of the outcome Jacobian** across the parameter sweep — how many
   independent directions the verb can actually move you in. A strong monotone
   dial has rank 1; a genuine expander has rank > 1.
3. **Differential entropy** of the outcome distribution rather than its spread.
4. **Pairwise distinguishability**: the count of outcome pairs separated by more
   than the measurement's own noise floor. Ties the metric to what is resolvable.
5. **A permutation null for expansion itself** [cheap] — shuffle the parameter
   labels and recompute. Whatever survives is manufactured by the estimator.
   *Revision B of the plan requires this before expansion becomes a gate.*
6. **Lever strength reported separately and always**, so the DRAG confusion can
   never recur silently.
7. **Compare against a DO-NOTHING sweep** where the "parameter" is a no-op —
   the true zero for expansion.
8. **Measure at several horizons** (150 / 600 / 2000 steps); an expander should
   grow the set with time, a lever should not.
9. **Reachable-set estimate by sampling many parameter combinations**, not one
   dial at a time — the plan's own "never one-factor-at-a-time" rule applied to
   the metric.
10. **Mutual information** between parameter and outcome vector: steerability
    without assuming monotonicity, which the rank correlation does assume.
11. **Report the metric's own noise floor** on every cell.
12. **A verb that provably cannot expand** (PIN, which removes a degree of
    freedom) as the negative control the expansion metric must score at zero.

## B · Verb candidates that might genuinely expand (P1, P2) — 13–26

13. **Mass injection** — the audit's lead, measured best-paired (0.0632 / 0.983).
14. **Mass injection with CHOSEN placement**, not just chosen size — position is
    the second axis and the one the Jacobian rank test would detect.
15. **Non-commuting verb PAIRS.** [cheap] Borrowed from quantum control: the
    reachable set of a bilinear system is generated by the *Lie algebra* of its
    fields. Two fields whose bracket is non-zero reach states neither reaches
    alone. **Predicts** two impulses on the same body along the same axis add
    nothing, while an impulse plus a displaced mass compound.
16. **Resonant nudge** — a small periodic impulse at an orbital frequency.
    Expansion bought with *timing precision* rather than magnitude, which makes
    skill the currency.
17. **Subtraction: remove a body.** Deletion opens configurations that body's
    gravity forbade.
18. **Tidal disruption** — bring two masses close enough to tear a third. The
    expansion lives in the near-singular encounters ε bounds.
19. **ε as a player-facing dial** rather than a constant: "focus" that sharpens
    interactions locally at the cost of predictability.
20. **Angular momentum injection** (a torque couple) rather than linear impulse —
    a different conserved quantity, so a different reachable direction.
21. **Split a body into two.** Conserves mass and momentum, adds a degree of
    freedom, and is visually unambiguous.
22. **Merge two bodies.** The inverse; removes a degree of freedom but releases
    binding energy.
23. **A massless probe** the player launches — changes nothing physically, and
    therefore tests P4 directly: does the player value *information* alone?
24. **Phase-shift**: displace one body along its own orbit without changing its
    energy. Pure geometry, zero energetics.
25. **Inject a body with RETROGRADE motion** — opposes the disc's rotation and is
    the single cheapest way to raise the encounter rate.
26. **A temporary attractor placed and then removed**, so the intervention has a
    duration as well as a magnitude.

## C · Objectives with real tension (P2) — 27–38

The measured problem: mass injection, drag and competent inaction all raise
boundness. A game whose verbs agree with doing nothing has no decision in it.

27. **Sculpt, don't preserve.** The target is a *shape* — a ring, a bar, two
    lobes — so "more bound" is not automatically better.
28. **Hit a specified final configuration** within a tolerance. Makes the
    objective vector-valued, which also fixes P1.
29. **Deliver a body to a place at a time.** Orbital mechanics as a courier
    problem.
30. **Maximise encounters without losing the cluster** — two quantities that pull
    opposite ways, the dual-constraint shape the other two games already use.
31. **Harvest**: energy released by mergers, spent on further interventions.
32. **Survive a perturbation you are told is coming**, at a known time.
33. **Keep N bodies bound while ejecting exactly one.**
34. **A budget of intervention impulse** for the whole round, so every verb
    competes with every other.
35. **Score the PATH, not the endpoint** — reward the system for passing through
    a named configuration, which no endpoint metric can capture.
36. **Two-phase round**: arrange, then release and watch. Separates planning from
    execution explicitly.
37. **Predict your own outcome** — commit to where a body will be, score the
    error. Makes the prediction horizon the scoring surface.
38. **Asymmetric co-op**: one player perturbs, one predicts.

## D · Legibility in a still frame (P3, P4) — 39–48

39. **Camera elevation** — done, and it was the defect. [shipped]
40. **Trails**, without which a photograph of an orbit and of a standstill are
    the same picture. [shipped]
41. **A forecast cone** per body — the envelope of where it will be, widening
    with the Lyapunov horizon. The IR/UV forecast mechanic, in its natural
    3D form.
42. **The cone IS the fairness readout**: it visibly widens as λ·t grows, so the
    player can see how far ahead prediction is trustworthy.
43. **Depth by desaturation, never brightness alone** — the two-axis separation
    that fixed Farkle's dice. [shipped]
44. **A ground-plane grid** so depth has a reference. [shipped]
45. **UV for intention** (the aim reticle, the declared target), **IR for
    hazard** (imminent encounters, escape trajectories), matter for what IS.
46. **Render the reachable set itself** — a translucent hull of where the system
    *could* go. Makes P1's metric the picture.
47. **Sonify the encounter rate** — pitch rising as bodies approach, which is
    information the eye misses in a cluttered frame.
48. **A 4D level as a factor with a comprehension gate**, per the user's own
    constraint: if it cannot be explained in one sentence a player acts on
    correctly, it loses to 3D regardless of measured depth.

## E · Fairness, exploits, determinism (already partly built) — 49–55

49. **Q32.32 fixed point** throughout. [shipped]
50. **Round-to-nearest-even**, so rounding error is zero-mean. [shipped]
51. **Velocity Verlet**, symplectic, bounded oscillatory energy error. [shipped]
52. **N8 directional-drift test** on energy, linear and angular momentum.
53. **A glitch-hunter** maximising drift in ANY conserved quantity, not just
    energy — the approving audit's refinement.
54. **Viability oracle** with its **rejection rate gated at 1–20%**, and the
    rejected seeds characterised rather than discarded.
55. **Solution-space variance** as the generator's health metric, since rejection
    rate measures solvability rather than diversity.

|---|---|---|
| 1 | **#5 + #1** permutation null and vector outcome | until expansion is measurable, every verb result is uninterpretable |
| 2 | **#15** non-commuting pairs | a borrowed structural prediction that is cheap and falsifiable |
| 3 | **#27/#28** sculpt a shape | attacks P2 directly; the current objective has no tension |
| 4 | **#18/#19** tidal / ε sweep | discriminates two tail rows at once |
| 5 | **#41** forecast cone | makes the Lyapunov horizon the interface |

**Not to be repeated from Chemical:** anything whose whole effect is showing the
player more. Observability is the preview dial, and it trades solver margin
one-for-one.
